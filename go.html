AHR plays 9×9 Go.
Here is what it did and did not win.

A compact (1,053,799-parameter) teacher-trained network, searched with PUCT plus AHR's bounded tactical reading, was benchmarked against GNU Go and KataGo on 9×9 with 7.5 komi. It beats GNU Go at every level tested and loses every game to KataGo. Both results are shown below.

How to read these results

  • Teacher-dependent. The network was initialised by learning from KataGo-generated policy and value targets. This is not evidence that AHR can reach this strength through its own self-play.
  • GNU Go is a weak opponent by modern standards. Beating level 10 is a real result for a network this small, but it does not imply amateur-dan, Elo or human-rank equivalence.
  • The KataGo gap is large. A separate earlier model line recorded 3/100 against KataGo at one visit. Neither result estimates the compute needed to close the gap.
  • Attribution is open. The benchmark did not isolate the contribution of the energy parameterisation, tactical priors, bounded reading or verification scheduling from standard neural tree search.
  • Development-set results. Checkpoint selection used a development set; no sealed external test or independent replication exists.
  • Soft time budget. The nominal 2 s per move was a soft target; 93.24% of moves exceeded it (mean 2.009 s).
  • Reconstructed record. The per-opponent counts were reconciled from the retained diagnostic output and worker aggregate. The original game-by-game report was not available to re-hash.
